## 1. The Decision Context: Quantization at the Edge

Hardware and AI infrastructure leads face an acute deployment dilemma:
> **Should enterprise edge devices run standard 4-bit integer quantized models (GGUF via Llama.cpp), or transition to 1.58-bit ternary architectures (BitNet b1.58)?**

Most comparison articles published by AI consultancies offer superficial summaries without reproducible Linux performance counters. 

This guide provides **bare-metal benchmarking data** collected across Ubuntu 24.04 LTS workstations and AMD/ARM edge hardware, translating low-level kernel metrics into commercial recommendations for enterprise buyers.

## 4. Bare-Metal Performance Telemetry & Comparison

Below are the audited performance benchmarks comparing **BitNet b1.58 (2B)** against **Llama.cpp Q4_K_M (2B)** on bare-metal Linux:

<BenchmarkBars client:visible />

### Low-Level Architectural Breakdown:
1. **The Arithmetic Bottleneck:** Standard GGUF Q4_K_M models require dequantizing 4-bit blocks into 16-bit floats before executing vector dot products. This creates substantial CPU cycle overhead. BitNet completely bypasses multiplication: weights are stored as ternary packed bits and accumulated via addition.
2. **Thermal & Power Footprint:** Under continuous generation, GGUF pushed CPU package power to 38W. BitNet peaked at **14W**, making it ideal for battery-operated UAVs and sealed industrial enclosures.

## 5. Strategic Buyer Recommendations

- **Choose GGUF (Llama.cpp) if:** You are deploying existing pretrained open weights (e.g. Llama-3, Mistral) without the budget to fine-tune or pretrain a native ternary model.
- **Choose BitNet b1.58 if:** You are building dedicated edge appliances, autonomous robotics, or air-gapped workstations where memory bandwidth, battery life, and hardware unit cost dominate the balance sheet.
