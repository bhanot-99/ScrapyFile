## 1. The Decision Context: Quantization at the Edge

Hardware and AI infrastructure leads face a deployment dilemma:
> **Should enterprise edge devices run standard 4-bit integer quantized models (GGUF via Llama.cpp), or transition to 1.58-bit ternary architectures (BitNet b1.58)?**

Most comparison articles published by marketing agencies offer superficial summaries without architectural depth. 

This guide examines the theoretical and low-level arithmetic trade-offs between standard dequantize-and-multiply pipelines and addition-only ternary kernels.

### Low-Level Architectural Breakdown:
1. **The Arithmetic Bottleneck:** Standard GGUF Q4_K_M models require dequantizing 4-bit blocks into floating-point numbers before executing vector dot products. This creates CPU cycle overhead. BitNet eliminates float multiplication: weights are stored as ternary packed bits and accumulated via addition.
2. **Thermal & Power Footprint:** By reducing floating-point MAC (multiply-accumulate) operations, ternary models reduce energy per token, making them well-suited for constrained thermal envelopes.

## 5. Strategic Buyer Recommendations

- **Choose GGUF (Llama.cpp) if:** You are deploying existing pretrained open weights (e.g. Llama-3, Mistral) without the budget to fine-tune or pretrain a native ternary model.
- **Choose BitNet b1.58 if:** You are building dedicated edge appliances or air-gapped workstations where memory bandwidth, power constraints, and unit cost dominate the balance sheet.
